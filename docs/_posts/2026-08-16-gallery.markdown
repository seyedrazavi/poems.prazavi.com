Some come to gather masterpieces;
others stand still until the paintings claim them.
The walls have practised crimson for centuries,
warming each passing face with their borrowed blood.
A man in black sits without haste,
his notebook shut, his eyes working beyond words.
Pilgrims orbit frame after frame,
swallowing beauty one small label at a time.
He stays until the portraits lose their century,
and looking becomes the gaze that erases distance.
